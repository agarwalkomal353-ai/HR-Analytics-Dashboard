
# HR Employee Attrition Analysis

## 📌 Project Overview

This project analyzes employee attrition patterns using the IBM HR Analytics Employee Attrition & Performance dataset.

The objective of this project is to understand employee attrition and identify patterns related to factors such as overtime, job role, tenure, job satisfaction, work-life balance, business travel, job level, and income.

The analysis was performed using Microsoft Excel, and an interactive HTML dashboard was developed with the assistance of AI tools to present the key findings in a clear and professional way.

---

## 🎯 Project Objectives

- Analyze overall employee attrition.
- Identify departments and job roles with different attrition levels.
- Analyze the relationship between overtime and employee attrition.
- Understand attrition patterns across job satisfaction and work-life balance.
- Analyze attrition based on employee age and tenure.
- Compare employees who stayed with those who left.
- Present the findings through an interactive dashboard and analytical report.

## 🔍 Key Analysis

The project analyzes employee attrition across several dimensions:

### Overall Attrition

- Total Employees: *1,470*
- Employees Who Left: *237*
- Overall Attrition Rate: *16.1%*

### Overtime

Employees working overtime showed a higher observed attrition rate:

- Overtime: *30.5%*
- No Overtime: *10.4%*

### Job Role

Attrition varied considerably across job roles.

- Sales Representatives: *39.8%*
- Laboratory Technicians: *23.9%*
- Human Resources: *23.1%*
- Research Directors: *2.5%*

### Employee Tenure

Employees with shorter tenure showed higher observed attrition:

- Less than 2 years: *34.9%*
- More than 10 years: *8.1%*

### Work-Life Balance

Employees with a work-life balance rating of 1 had an observed attrition rate of *31.2%*.

## 💡 Key Takeaways

The analysis identified several notable patterns in employee attrition:

- Overall employee attrition was *16.1%*.
- Attrition was substantially higher among employees working overtime.
- Employees with shorter tenure showed higher observed attrition.
- Attrition varied significantly across job roles.
- Lower work-life balance ratings were associated with higher observed attrition.

These findings represent patterns observed in the dataset and should not be interpreted as proof of causal relationships.
